Fix My Job is an independent app. It is not a government app and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or acting for any government agency.

Fix My Job helps people who work with their hands — in warehouses, delivery, driving, care, cleaning, factories, construction and maintenance — get paid what they're worth and find their next step. In English and Spanish.

COMPARE YOUR PAY
See where you stand next to others in the same job in your state: the middle of the pay range, what the top quarter earns, and how far you are from either.

EARN MORE
Certifications and licenses that pay off in your job, training programs near you, and what people who complete them typically earn.

GET PROMOTED
Realistic next moves from your job — supervisor roles, better-paid related jobs or a new career — with the steps for each: licenses, skills and training, cheapest first.

KNOW YOUR RIGHTS
Check your state's minimum wage, overtime, breaks, paid sick leave, and whether you may be treated as a contractor when you should be an employee. If something looks wrong, see what you may be owed and what to do next.

ONE SIMPLE PLAN
Quick wins and big moves in one list. Tick off steps and share your plan.

WHERE THE INFORMATION COMES FROM
Every figure in the app shows its source and year. Figures we calculate from public data are marked as Fix My Job estimates. These are all of our sources:

Government sources
• Pay by job and state, union pay: US Bureau of Labor Statistics — https://www.bls.gov
• Pay with and without a license, earnings after training, ZIP code to city: US Census Bureau — https://www.census.gov
• Jobs, skills and related occupations: O*NET, US Department of Labor — https://www.onetcenter.org
• Cost of living: US Bureau of Economic Analysis — https://www.bea.gov
• Approved training programs and results: US Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration — https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta
• College programs, tuition and earnings: US Department of Education — https://collegescorecard.ed.gov
• American Job Centers: CareerOneStop, US Department of Labor — https://www.careeronestop.org
• Licenses a job requires (FMCSA, CMS, EPA, OSHA, state licensing boards): CareerOneStop — https://www.careeronestop.org/Toolkit/Training/find-licenses.aspx
• State minimum wage and overtime: US Department of Labor, Wage and Hour Division — https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/minimum-wage/state
• State labor offices and local minimum wages (city and county governments, state labor departments) — https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/contacts
• Meal breaks, rest breaks and reporting-time pay (state labor laws) — https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/meal-breaks
• Paid sick leave: state labor departments and US Department of Labor, Women's Bureau — https://www.dol.gov/agencies/wb
• Workplace safety State Plans: OSHA — https://www.osha.gov/stateplans
• Federal labor laws (FLSA, OSHA, FMLA, NLRA, ADA, ADEA, Title VII): US Department of Labor, EEOC and NLRB — https://www.dol.gov/general/aboutdol/majorlaws
• Overtime salary level and payroll tax rates: US Department of Labor and Internal Revenue Service — https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc751

Other sources (not government)
• Online courses: each training provider's own website, linked on every course
• More courses, and the licenses and certifications they prepare for: Credential Engine (Credential Registry, nonprofit) — https://credentialfinder.org
• Union directory: national union websites and the AFL-CIO — https://aflcio.org/formaunion

This is the complete list. You can also see it in the app under Menu → Data sources.

YOUR DATA
We never contact your employer. You can ask us to delete your data at any time from the app's menu.
