  At $35,600 you're earning about $17/hr if you work full time. That's roughly average for Memphis. Here are the options, from biggest raise to smallest.
  
  ## 1. Become an LPN (the best next step)
  - ZipRecruiter lists the average LPN pay in Memphis at $55,463 a year. Indeed's average is $26.97 per hour.
  - Glassdoor reports higher figures. Its average is $63,705 per year.
  - Sources disagree, but all of them put LPN pay well above what you make now. A realistic expectation is a jump of about $15,000–$25,000 a year.
  - LPN programs typically take 12–18 months. Community college tuition is lower than you might expect. For example, some community college LPN programs list in-state tuition around $3,300–$3,740. Those two are in Arkansas and Mississippi, so check Tennessee options too, such as Southwest Tennessee Community College and Concorde.
  - Your 1.5 years of experience will help your application and make the coursework easier.
  
  ## 2. Use employer tuition help
  Ask your HR department about this first, because it could cut your costs a lot.
  - Methodist Le Bonheur offers tuition assistance after six months of continuous employment. It also has LPN and RN residency programs and an LPN-to-RN associate degree pathway.
  - Methodist has also advertised student loan repayment of up to $25,000 for continuing to RN. That listing is a couple of years old, so confirm the current terms.
  - Baptist Memorial and Regional One Health are also worth asking. A university page suggests checking their careers pages for tuition reimbursement or discount programs.
  - You may also qualify for free or reduced-cost training through Tennessee's WIOA workforce programs. Your local American Job Center can tell you.
  
  ## 3. Move to a hospital if you're in a nursing home or home health
  One local training-school source says hospital systems pay Memphis CNAs the most, with wages 15–25% above nursing-home rates. It reports Methodist CNAs earning roughly $19–$24/hour with shift differentials. That's a school's marketing page, so treat the numbers as approximate. Even so, $19/hr would be about $4,000 more a year than you make now.
  
  ## 4. Raise your pay as a CNA while you study
  - Work nights, weekends, or PRN shifts. Shift differentials and per-diem pay can add up quickly.
  - Look for specialty units such as ICU, dialysis, or behavioral health, or for CNA II/Med Tech roles. These often pay more.
  - Ask for a raise or review. After 1.5 years you have a case, especially with good attendance and evaluations.
  
  ## 5. Longer term: RN
  Many LPNs later move on to RN through a bridge program, often with employer help. That is where pay grows most over a career. You don't have to decide now, but picking a path with a bridge program keeps the option open.
  
  ## A practical plan
  1. This month, ask HR what tuition assistance exists for your current employer. Also compare it with Methodist, Baptist, and Regional One.
  2. Look into LPN prerequisites and the application deadlines at two or three local programs.
  3. Fill out the FAFSA. It's free, and you may qualify for Pell Grants.
  4. In the meantime, pick up extra shifts or move to a higher-paying employer.
  
  If you tell me more, I can tailor this. It would help to know your current workplace (hospital, nursing home or home health), your schedule, whether you have dependents, and whether you want to keep working while in school.
